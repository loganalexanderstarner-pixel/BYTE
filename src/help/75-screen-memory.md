This is the most careful helper, so it is last and **off until you turn it on**: [Settings → Control and screen](byte-setting:control) → **Everyday helpers** → **Searchable memory of my screen**.

## What it does
Every few minutes (you choose how often), while you are at the computer, BYTE reads the **words** of the window in front and keeps **only those words**, with the time, the program and the window title. Later you can search them: type in the search box in Settings, or ask "what was I looking at about solar panels?" or "where did I see the flight number?". BYTE lists the matches itself, from the saved text.

## What it never does
- It never keeps a **picture**. A picture is made in memory, turned into words and dropped.
- It never leaves this computer, and it uses **no model**: the answers are a text search, so nothing can reach BYTE Cloud.
- It skips windows on your never-look-at list and on its own **Never remember** list, password managers, banks, private browser windows, BYTE itself, private chats, kids mode, full-screen games, a locked BYTE, and times you are away from the keyboard.
- Card-like numbers and key-like words are blanked before the text is kept.
- The tray icon's tooltip says it is on.

## Control
- **How long it keeps things:** 1 to 90 days, or **Until I clear it**. Older text is deleted by itself.
- **Forget the last hour** deletes the recent part.
- **Clear everything** deletes it all at once, and gives the space back.
- Turning the switch off stops it at once; what was already kept stays until you clear it or it expires.
